Mary: God's Surprising Plan

Intro – A Surprise in a Box
5 min

Hold up a wrapped box. Let a couple of children shake it and guess what’s inside — is it big? small? noisy? Build the excitement, then open it together and reveal what’s inside.

Then say: “That was a fun surprise! But today we’re going to hear about the biggest surprise anyone has ever had. God had a surprise for a young girl named Mary. It was a plan so surprising that at first it made her a little bit scared.”

Ask the group: “Has anyone ever asked you to do something that felt surprising, or a bit scary?” (Let a few share — affirm every response, then lead into the story.)
Before class: wrap a box and place something simple inside — a small toy, or a baby doll to hint at where the story is heading.

Bible Lesson – God’s Story: Mary
10 min

Bible passage: Luke 1:26–38

Watch the video together, then talk through the story and the Jesus Connection before praying.

If the video doesn’t load, open it here: youtu.be/i2YBd_88vRQ

The Story in Brief

Out of everyone in the whole world, God chose an ordinary young woman named Mary to be Jesus’ mother. An angel appeared to her with a surprising message: she would give birth to the Rescuer God’s family had been waiting for. At first Mary was terrified — but the angel told her God had chosen her for a reason and she didn’t need to be afraid. Even though God’s plan didn’t make sense yet, Mary trusted Him, and she sang a song calling God her Saviour. Mary went on to raise Jesus, watched Him do amazing miracles, saw Him die on the cross, and then saw Him alive again three days later. She never kept the good news to herself — she helped share the story of Jesus with others.

Jesus Connection: Mary was brave and she trusted God, even when His plan was surprising and didn’t make sense yet. But Mary needed a Saviour too. When the angel came, Mary sang a song and called God “my Saviour” — because Mary knew she wasn’t perfect either. The baby Mary carried grew up to be the one who would take the punishment for Mary’s sin, and yours, and mine, when He died on the cross. Mary got to hold the Saviour in her arms. We get to hold Him in our hearts.

🙏 Prayer

Dear God, thank You for Your surprising plan. Thank You that Mary trusted You even when it was scary. Help us to trust You and say yes to You, just like Mary did. And thank You most of all for sending Jesus, our Saviour. Amen.

Game – True or False?
5 min

A quick, active recap of the video. Put the TRUE sign on one wall and the FALSE sign on the opposite wall. Read each statement aloud from the leader card — the children run to the sign they think is right, then you confirm the answer and fill in what really happened.

  • The leader card lists all twelve statements in story order, with the answer and a short correction under each false one, so you can walk the children back through the whole video as you go.
  • Short on time? Drop a few statements — the first three make the gentlest warm-up.
Before class: print the TRUE and FALSE signs (pages 1 and 2) and tape them to opposite walls. Print the leader card (page 3) for yourself.

Craft – Mary Triorama
10 min

A 3D standing scene of the moment the angel came to Mary, with a Mary figure that stands in front of it.

Before the Session

  • Print the craft page onto A4 cardstock — one per child.
  • For the youngest children, pre-cut the outside square and the diagonal slit so they can go straight to colouring, gluing, and standing it up.

Materials

  • Printed craft page on A4 cardstock — one per child
  • Colouring pencils or crayons
  • Scissors
  • Glue sticks

Steps

  1. Colour the scene — Mary’s house, the angel, and the pottery on the shelves — and colour the Mary figure at the bottom of the page.
  2. Cut around the outside of the large square.
  3. Cut along the diagonal dashed line, from the corner in to the centre of the square (stop at the centre).
  4. Overlap the two bottom triangles (the tiled floor) and glue them together so the scene pops up and stands in a 3D corner.
  5. Cut out the Mary figure, fold along the tab, and stand her in front of the scene.
Leader note: as children finish, ask them to retell the moment the angel surprised Mary — a low-pressure way to recap the story while they work.
