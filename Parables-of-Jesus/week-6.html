The Pharisee & the Tax Collector

Ages 4–10 · 35 min

👋
Intro – What Makes a Good Prayer?
5 min

We’re still journeying through the Parables of Jesus — the short, everyday stories Jesus told to teach us something big and true about God. This week’s parable is all about prayer, and it has a surprise ending.

Start by getting the children thinking about talking to God.

Ask the group: “Who do we talk to when we pray?” (God! And He really hears every single prayer.)
Ask: “When we pray, do we have to use big fancy words and sound really good?” (Take a few answers.)
Ask: “Do you think there’s a right way and a wrong way to come to God?” (Let them wonder about it.)
Leader note: Today Jesus tells a story about two men who go to pray in very different ways — one proud, one humble. The surprise is who God is pleased with. Jesus told this parable for people who were sure they were good enough on their own and looked down on everyone else. It points us straight to the good news: we don’t come to God because we’re good enough — we come because He is merciful.
🎮
Game – Balloon Ping Pong
5 min

A quick, active game to open the session. It sets up today’s big idea: prayer isn’t just talking at God and walking away — it’s a real back-and-forth with a God who hears and answers.

Setup

  1. Blow up one balloon (keep a couple of spares handy).
  2. Clear a space, or set a simple “net” — a row of chairs or a length of string across the middle.
  3. Children can use their hands, or make paper-plate bats if you have them.

How to Play

  • Pair the children up (or stand in a circle) and keep the balloon in the air, tapping it back and forth — it takes both sides to keep it going.
  • See how long the group can keep the balloon up without it touching the floor.
Make the connection: Just like the balloon needs both sides, prayer goes two ways — we talk to God, and God hears and answers. In a moment we’ll meet two men who prayed in very different ways, and see whose prayer God was pleased with.

Bible Lesson – The Pharisee & the Tax Collector
10 min

Bible passage: Luke 18:9–14

Watch the video together, then play the true-or-false recap below.

True or False – Run to the Wall

Put the True sign on one wall and the False sign on the opposite wall. Read out a statement about the story: children run to the side they think is correct. After each one, reveal the answer and say the quick reason.

⬇ Download the True/False Wall Signs (PDF)
1. Jesus told this story to people who were proud and thought they were better than everyone else.
TRUE — He wanted to show them what God really thinks of a proud heart.
2. The two men went to the Temple to pray.
TRUE — one was a Pharisee (a very religious man) and the other was a tax collector.
3. The Pharisee’s prayer was all about how good he was.
TRUE — he thanked God that he wasn’t a sinner like other people, especially that tax collector.
4. The tax collector marched right up the front and prayed nice and loud.
FALSE — he stood far off, kept his head down, and was too sorry to even look up to heaven.
5. The tax collector asked God to be merciful to him because he knew he was a sinner.
TRUE — his prayer was short and honest: “God, be merciful to me, a sinner!”
6. God was more pleased with the proud Pharisee than the humble tax collector.
FALSE — it was the tax collector who went home right with God, not the Pharisee.
7. Jesus said if you make yourself proud and big, you will be brought low.
TRUE — “but the one who humbles himself will be lifted up.”
8. We can make ourselves good enough for God just by doing lots of good things.
FALSE — none of us can earn our way to God. We all need His mercy, and Jesus is the One who makes us right with God.

Jesus Connection: The Pharisee thought all his good deeds made him good enough for God. But God doesn’t count us right because we tick a list of good things — if He did, none of us would ever measure up. The tax collector had nothing to offer but an honest, humble heart: “God, be merciful to me.” And that’s exactly the man God forgave. Here’s the good news for us: Jesus lived the perfect life we could never live, then died and rose again so that everyone who comes to God humbly, trusting Him, is forgiven and made right with God. We don’t come proud of ourselves — we come trusting Jesus.

🙏 Prayer

Dear God, thank You that we don’t have to pretend to be good enough to come to You. Thank You for Jesus, who died and rose again so we can be forgiven. Please be merciful to us. Help us come to You with humble, honest hearts, just like the tax collector. Amen.

Craft – Fold-Up Mini Book
10 min
Leader note: Start the background playlist here and let it play quietly while the children colour and fold their mini books.

    Each child makes their own little book that retells the parable — to colour, fold, and take home, so the story can be read again during the week.

    Before the Session

    • Print one mini book per child. The download has a colour-in version (page 1) and a ready-coloured version (page 2) — choose whichever suits your group.
    • The last page of the download is a simple fold-and-cut guide — keep one handy to show the children.

    Materials (per child)

    • One printed mini-book sheet
    • Colouring pencils or crayons
    • Scissors (an adult makes the single centre cut)
    • A pen for writing their name

    Steps

    1. Colour in the pictures.
    2. Fold the sheet in half three times, then unfold to find the eight pages.
    3. Fold in half and (with adult help) cut the short slit in the centre.
    4. Open out, then push the ends together and fold into a little book.
    5. Write your name on the “This Book Belongs to” page, then read it through together.
