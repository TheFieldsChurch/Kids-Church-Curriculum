Parables of Jesus · Week 4

The Lost Sheep

Ages 4–10 · 35 min
Memory Verse: “The Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost.” — Luke 19:10 (ESV)
👋
Intro – The One That’s Missing
5 min

Welcome back! So far we’ve met the farmer scattering seed (and how God wants His word to grow deep roots in us), the wise and foolish builders (building our lives on Jesus, our rock), and the tiny mustard seed (how God grows big things from small beginnings). This week Jesus tells a parable about a shepherd who loses just one sheep — and how far he goes to find it. Get the children thinking about losing something precious before the story begins.

Ask the group: “Have you ever lost something you really love — a toy, a pet, or even got lost yourself for a moment?” (Let a few children share.)
Ask: “How did you feel when it was missing? And how did you feel when it was found again?” (Take a few answers.)
Ask: “If you had a hundred toys and lost just one, would you still go looking for it? Why?” (Take a few answers.)
Leader note: Today Jesus tells a story about a shepherd with a hundred sheep. When one wanders off, he leaves the ninety-nine and searches until he finds it — then carries it home with joy. The surprise is that it isn’t really about sheep: it’s about how much God loves every single person, and how Jesus, the Good Shepherd, came to seek and save the lost.
🎮
Game – The Lost Sheep Hunt
5 min

An active hide-and-seek before the story. The children become the shepherds, hunting all around the room for the little sheep that have wandered off — and cheering when the one special “lost sheep” is found and carried home to the pen. That celebration sets up the whole parable we’re about to hear.

Before the Session

  1. Print and cut out the paper sheep (below) and hide them around the room — one or two per child. Tuck them behind chairs, under cushions, on windowsills.
  2. Hide the one special golden “lost sheep” a little harder than the rest.
  3. Set the sheep pen sign on a box or in a corner — this is where found sheep come home.
  4. Have the Good and Faithful Shepherd song (below) ready as the search music.
🎵 Good and Faithful Shepherd — search music
Play the song while the shepherds search; when it stops, everyone brings their sheep back to the pen.

How to Play

  • Explain: “A shepherd has lost his sheep! You are the shepherds — go and find every one, and bring them safely to the pen.”
  • Start the music and let the children hunt for the hidden sheep around the room.
  • Each time a child finds a sheep, they carry it back and add it to the pen.
  • The big moment is the one golden lost sheep — when it’s found, everyone cheers and celebrates together: “The lost sheep is found!”
Leader tip: For a calmer version with little ones, hide just one sheep and play warmer / colder as they search. That joyful moment of finding the lost sheep is the perfect hand-off straight into the Bible story.

Bible Lesson – The Lost Sheep
10 min

Bible passage: Luke 15:1–7 (also Matthew 18:10–14)

Watch the video together, then play the true-or-false recap below.

▶︎ Watch the Bible Story Video
Leader note: Preview the video before the session to make sure you’re happy with it — if you’d prefer a different one, any short kids’ “Parable of the Lost Sheep” video will fit here.

True or False – Stand Up / Stay Sitting

Read out a statement about the story: children stand up if they think it’s true and stay sitting if they think it’s false. After each one, reveal the answer and say the quick reason.

1. Jesus told a story about a shepherd who had one hundred sheep.
TRUE — the shepherd looked after a whole flock of a hundred sheep.
2. One of the sheep wandered off and got lost.
TRUE — one little sheep strayed away from all the others.
3. The shepherd didn’t care about the one lost sheep because he still had lots of others.
FALSE — he cared very much, and went straight out to look for it.
4. The shepherd left the ninety-nine and went to search for the one lost sheep.
TRUE — he left the ninety-nine safe and went after the missing one.
5. The shepherd looked for a little while, then gave up.
FALSE — he searched and searched until he found it.
6. When he found his sheep, the shepherd was sad and grumpy.
FALSE — he was full of joy and carried it home on his shoulders.
7. The shepherd called his friends together to celebrate that his sheep was found.
TRUE — “Rejoice with me, for I have found my lost sheep!”
8. Jesus is like the Good Shepherd, who comes to find us when we are lost.
TRUE — and all of heaven celebrates when even one person comes home to God.

Jesus Connection: The shepherd wouldn’t stop until he found his one lost sheep — that’s how much God loves you. Jesus is the Good Shepherd who came to seek and to save the lost, and He gave His own life to bring us home. You are so precious to God that He would search until He found you — and when even one person turns back to Him, all of heaven throws a party. So you never need to feel too small or too far away: the Good Shepherd is always coming to find you.

🙏 Prayer

Dear God, thank You that You are the Good Shepherd who never gives up on us. Thank You that You love every single one of us, and that You came to seek and to save the lost. When we wander, help us to hear Your voice and come home to You. Thank You that all of heaven celebrates when even one of us turns back to You. Thank You for Jesus. Amen.

Craft – My Little Found Sheep
10 min
Leader note: Start the background playlist here and let it play quietly while the children work on their craft.

    Each child makes a fluffy cotton-wool sheep to take home, with the memory verse on a little tag — a soft reminder that Jesus, the Good Shepherd, came to find us.

    Time-saver: Cut out the sheep templates before the session if you have lots of little ones — then the children just glue on the cotton wool, colour the face, and write their name.

    Before the Session

    • Print the sheep template (below) onto stiff card, one per child, and cut them out (or let older children cut their own).
    • Pull a bag of cotton wool balls apart into fluffy pieces so they’re ready to glue.
    • Set out glue sticks or PVA, and coloured markers for the face and legs.

    Materials (per child)

    • One printed “Found Sheep” template on stiff card
    • A handful of cotton wool balls
    • A glue stick or PVA
    • Coloured markers

    Steps

    1. Glue cotton wool all over the sheep’s fluffy body until it’s nice and woolly.
    2. Colour in the sheep’s face and legs with the markers.
    3. Write your name on the memory-verse tag.
    4. Say the memory verse together while everyone finishes: “The Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost.”
    5. Take your little found sheep home and stand it somewhere you’ll see it — a reminder that the Good Shepherd came to find you.

    “Found Sheep” Template & Verse Tags

    Print on stiff card, one per child (or one sheet makes two). Cut around the sheep, glue on cotton wool, and fill in the name tag.
