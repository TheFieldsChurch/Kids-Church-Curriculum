Intro – Hero Hall of Fame
5 min

Hold up one craft at a time — the ones the children made themselves over the last five weeks — and let them shout out which hero it belongs to. Move quickly; this is a fast, noisy recap of the whole series.

  • Noah’s Ark Spinner (Week 1) — spin it from the storm round to the rainbow. Noah trusted God even when it seemed crazy.
  • David and Goliath Cup Launcher (Week 2) — David was small, but God is big.
  • Bible camera film strip (Week 3) — pull it through the frames. Esther was brave for a reason.
  • Lion head with the mane (Week 4) — Daniel kept praying, no matter what.
  • Mary triorama (Week 5) — Mary said yes to God’s surprising plan.

Then say: “Wow — look at all these heroes. Every single one of them was brave, and every single one of them trusted God. But here’s the thing: not one of them was perfect. Every one of them needed to be rescued too. So who were they all pointing to? Today we meet the greatest Hero of all.”

Ask the group: “What makes someone a hero?” (Let a few share — strong, brave, saves people. Affirm every answer, then say: “Today’s Hero is different. He didn’t rescue us by being strong. He rescued us by giving up His life for us.”)
Before class: dig out the sample crafts you made when preparing Weeks 1–5 and put them in a bag or box so you can pull them out one at a time. The children took their own copies home, so you will need your own set. If any are missing, print the template again from that week’s lesson page — it does not need to be coloured in, the children will recognise the shape. Working through them in order builds nicely to the question at the end.

Game – Rescue Relay
5 min

A game in two parts, both making the same point: we cannot rescue ourselves — someone has to come and do it for us. No equipment and no printables, just floor space.

Part 1 – Lift Yourself Up

Have everyone sit cross-legged on the floor and slide both hands palm-down underneath them, so they are sitting on their own hands. Now challenge them: “Without taking your hands out, try to lift yourself up off the ground.” Let them wriggle and strain for a few seconds — nobody can do it. Then say: “No matter how hard we try, we can’t lift ourselves up. And no matter how hard we try, we can’t rescue ourselves from the wrong things we do. We need someone else to do it for us.”

Part 2 – The Rescue

Spread the children out around the room and have each one “stuck” on the spot — feet frozen to the floor, unable to move. Tell them to try to reach the safe “home” wall on their own: they can stretch and reach, but they cannot move their feet, so they can’t get there. Then name one child (or start yourself) as the Rescuer. The Rescuer runs to a stuck child, takes them by the hand, and walks them safely home. Once home, that child becomes a rescuer too and goes back out for someone else — so the rescue spreads until the whole room is home and safe.

  • Keep it gentle — walking and hand-holding, not dragging or pulling.
  • With a small group, you be the only Rescuer and bring them home one at a time.
  • If space is tight, shrink the “stuck” area and make the home wall just a few steps away.
As you finish: gather the children and land the point in one line — “You couldn’t lift yourself up, and you couldn’t get home on your own. Someone had to come and rescue you. That is exactly what Jesus did for us. We couldn’t save ourselves, so Jesus came and did it for us.”

Bible Lesson – God’s Story: Jesus
10 min

Bible passage: Genesis 1–3; Matthew 27:32–28:10

Watch the video together, then work through the quiz and the Jesus Connection before praying.

If the video doesn’t load, open it here: youtu.be/8u-ICIHAmKk

The Story in Brief

This video tells the whole of God’s story in one go. In the beginning God made a perfect world — oceans, mountains, giraffes and jellyfish — with no sickness, no sadness and no death. He made Adam and Eve to live with Him there forever, and He called them very good. But then they disobeyed God, and that is when all the wrong things in the world began. Even so, God loved His people too much to leave them, so He made a rescue plan: one day He would send His own Son. God’s family waited hundreds and hundreds of years. They expected a mighty king or a powerful warrior — and the rescuer arrived as a little baby. Jesus was completely human and completely God, and He never once did anything wrong. When He grew up He did miracles: He fed five thousand people with five loaves and two fish, He calmed a storm, He walked on water, He healed the sick, and He even brought a dead man back to life. He forgave people’s sins too. But some people refused to believe He was God’s Son, and they wanted Him dead — and that is what happened. Jesus suffered and died on a cross, even though He had never done anything wrong. His family was brokenhearted. Then something incredible happened: Jesus did not stay dead. He came back to life. That was God’s plan all along.

Jesus Connection: For six weeks we have met heroes who were brave and who trusted God — but every one of them did wrong things too, and every one of them needed a rescuer. Jesus is the only Hero who never did anything wrong. Here is the amazing part: the punishment for all the wrong things we do should have been ours, but Jesus took it instead. He died in our place. That is why the empty tomb matters so much — it means the rescue worked. Jesus beat sin and death, and everyone who trusts Him is rescued forever. Noah, David, Esther, Daniel and Mary were all pointing to this. Jesus is the Ultimate Hero.

Craft – Hero Reveal Wheel
10 min

A comic-style card with a window in the front and a spinning wheel behind it. Each turn brings a different Bible Heroes character into the window — and the last turn lands on Jesus.

Before the Session

  • Print both craft pages onto A4 cardstock — one set per child.
  • For the youngest children, pre-cut the front card, the window, and the wheel so they can go straight to colouring and assembling.
  • Push the split pins partway through the centre dots ahead of time to save fiddly fingers.

Materials

  • Printed craft pages on A4 cardstock — one set per child
  • Split pins (brads) — one per child
  • Colouring pencils or crayons
  • Scissors

Steps

  1. Colour the front card — the comic border and the words “Who is the greatest Hero of all?”
  2. Colour the six panels on the wheel: Noah, David, Esther, Daniel, Mary, and the cross and empty tomb.
  3. Cut out the front card and cut out the window opening.
  4. Cut out the wheel.
  5. Place the wheel behind the front card, line up the two centre dots, and push a split pin through both. Open the legs out flat at the back.
  6. Turn the wheel and read each hero as they appear — finishing on Jesus, the Ultimate Hero.
Leader note: as children finish, ask them to spin through their wheel and tell you one thing about each hero — then ask what makes the last one different. A gentle way to recap all six weeks while they work.
