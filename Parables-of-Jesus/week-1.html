Parables of Jesus · Week 1

The Sower

Ages 4–10 · 35 min
Memory Verse: “The seed is the word of God.” — Luke 8:11 (ESV)
👋
Intro – What Makes Things Grow?
5 min

Welcome to a brand new series! For the next eight weeks we’re going to look at the Parables of Jesus. Explain it simply to the children: “A parable is a short, everyday story that Jesus told to teach us something big and true about God. On the outside it’s about ordinary things — farmers, seeds, sheep and families — but on the inside every one of them helps us understand God and points us to Jesus.”

Now get the children thinking about seeds and growing before the story begins. If you can, bring a few real seeds to pass around.

Ask the group: “Has anyone ever planted a seed and watched it grow?” (Let a few children share.)
Ask: “What does a little seed need so it can grow big and strong?” (Water, sunshine, good soil…)
Ask: “What do you think would happen if you dropped a seed on a hard path, or on rocks?” (Take a few answers.)
Leader note: Today Jesus tells a story about a farmer scattering seed. The surprise is that it’s not really about gardening at all — it’s about our hearts and God’s word. This is the very first parable of our series, and Jesus explains it Himself, so it shows us how to listen to every parable that follows.

True or False – Stand Up / Stay Sitting

Read out a statement about the story: children stand up if they think it’s true and stay sitting if they think it’s false. After each one, reveal the answer and say the quick reason.

1. Jesus taught people about God by telling stories called parables.
TRUE — a parable is a story Jesus told to help us understand God.
2. In the story, a farmer went out to plant seeds in his field.
TRUE — the farmer scattered seed all over his field.
3. Every single seed grew into a big, tall plant.
FALSE — birds ate some, some dried up on the rocks, and thorns choked others. Only the good soil grew.
4. Birds swooped down and ate the seeds that fell on the path.
TRUE — they were gobbled up before they could grow.
5. The seeds on the rocky ground grew nice deep roots.
FALSE — their roots couldn’t go deep, so the hot sun burned them up.
6. The thorns helped the little plants grow big and strong.
FALSE — the thorns grew thick and blocked out the sun and rain.
7. The seeds in the good soil grew into big plants and made lots more seeds.
TRUE — they grew strong and produced many times what was planted.
8. The farmer in the story is like Jesus, telling people about God’s rescue plan.
TRUE — and the people who trust and follow Jesus are like the seeds that grow in the good soil.

Jesus Connection: In this story, the farmer who plants the seeds is like Jesus, sharing God’s word with everyone. The good seed is God’s word, and our hearts are like the soil. God wants us to have soft, ready hearts that hear His word and let it grow. When we love and trust Jesus, His word grows in us like a strong, healthy plant — and that lasts forever. So what kind of soil is your heart today?

🙏 Prayer

Dear God, thank you for giving us your word like good seed. Please make our hearts like good soil — soft to hear you and ready to hold on tight — so your word grows deep roots in us. Thank you for Jesus, who died and rose again so we could have new life. Help us to grow in you. Amen.

Game – Four Soils Seed Toss
5 min

This week the game comes straight after the video and true-or-false. A quick, active game that sorts the four soils from Jesus’ story.

Setup

  1. Mark out four squares on the floor with masking tape.
  2. Place a picture in the middle of each square so the children can see which soil it is: Path, Rocky Ground, Thorns, and Good Soil.
  3. Mark a throwing line with tape about 1–2 metres away.
  4. Gather a pile of “seeds” — beanbags, pom-poms, or scrunched paper balls.

How to Play

  • Children take turns tossing a seed and calling out which soil square it lands in.
  • Only seeds that land in the Good Soil square score a point — just like in the parable, only the good soil grew a harvest!
  • As you play, ask what happens to seed on the path, the rocks, and in the thorns.
  • Reset and let everyone have a few turns if time allows.

Craft – Grow-Your-Own Seed Cup
10 min
Leader note: Start the background playlist here and let it play quietly while the children work on their craft.

    Each child plants a real seed to take home and watch grow — a living reminder that God wants His word to grow deep roots in us.

    Before the Session

    • Set out cotton wool balls (or a little potting soil) and fast-growing seeds — cress, beans, or sunflower work best.
    • Have a jug of water and a few spoons ready for scooping soil.

    Materials (per child)

    • Clear plastic cup or small pot
    • Cotton wool balls or a scoop of potting soil
    • 2–3 fast-growing seeds (cress, bean, or sunflower)
    • A little water
    • Sticker or paper strip to write their name and “Luke 8:11”

    Steps

    1. Fill the cup with cotton wool or soil — this is the “good soil”.
    2. Push 2–3 seeds gently down into it.
    3. Add a small splash of water (not too much!).
    4. Stick on the name label with the memory verse: “The seed is the word of God.”
    5. Send it home with a reminder to keep it in the light, water it a little each day, and watch it grow over the week.

    Memory Verse Labels

    Print, cut along the borders, and let each child stick one on their pot and write their name. One A4 sheet makes 18 labels.
