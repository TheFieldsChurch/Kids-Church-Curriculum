Intro – When Is It Hard to Forgive?
5 min

Get the children thinking about forgiveness before the story begins.

Ask the group: “Has anyone ever said sorry to you? How did it feel to forgive them?” (Let them share — affirm every response.)
Ask: “Is it ever hard to forgive someone? Why?” (Take a few answers.)
Ask: “How many times do you think we should forgive someone who keeps getting it wrong?” (Encourage a few responses — this is exactly what Peter asked Jesus!)
Leader note: Today Jesus tells a story about a servant who owed the king an enormous debt — more money than he could ever pay back. The king forgave the whole lot. But then that same servant refused to forgive a friend who owed him just a tiny amount. Jesus told this story to show us something huge: God has forgiven us so much, so we can forgive others too.

Game – Unforgiving Servant Bingo
5 min

A 3×3 picture bingo using the characters from today’s parable, so the people and moments land when they watch the story next.

How to Play

Give each child a bingo card and something to mark with (counters, stickers, or a pencil). Every card has a FREE square already covered. The leader draws a calling card from the pile, holds it up, and names the picture (“the master”, “the forgiven servant being unkind”) so pre-readers can join in. Children cover the matching square. The first to complete a full line — row, column, or diagonal — shouts “Bingo!”

Tip: As you call each picture, say one line about it — “the master, who forgave a huge debt” or “the forgiven servant being unkind to his friend.” By the time you watch the video, they’ll already know the cast of the story.

Bible Lesson – The Unforgiving Servant
10 min

Bible passage: Matthew 18:21–35

Watch the video together, then work through the discussion questions below.

▶︎ Open the Video on YouTube

The Story in Brief

Peter asked Jesus, “How many times should I forgive someone? Seven times?” Jesus said, “Not seven times, but seventy-seven times” — meaning more than you can count. Then He told a story. A king wanted to settle his accounts. One servant owed him ten thousand bags of gold — a debt so big he could never repay it. The servant begged, “Please be patient with me!” The king felt sorry for him and forgave the whole debt. But that same servant went out, found a friend who owed him just a hundred silver coins, grabbed him, and had him thrown into prison. When the king heard, he was furious: “I forgave you everything. Shouldn’t you have had mercy on your friend, just as I had mercy on you?”

Discussion Questions

1. How big was the debt the king forgave? Could the servant ever have paid it back?
Sample answer: Ten thousand bags of gold — a huge amount he could never repay. The king forgave all of it, just because he felt sorry for the servant.
2. What did the forgiven servant do to his friend who owed him a little?
Sample answer: He grabbed him, refused to be patient, and had him thrown in prison — even though he himself had just been forgiven so much more.
3. Why was the king so angry at the end? What is Jesus teaching us?
Sample answer: The servant took the king’s mercy but wouldn’t show any himself. God has forgiven us far more than anyone could ever owe us — so He wants us to forgive others from the heart.

Jesus Connection: We are like that servant — we owe God a debt of sin we could never repay. But Jesus paid it for us on the cross. He took the punishment we deserved so that God could forgive us completely, once and for all. When we really understand how much we’ve been forgiven, it changes how we treat everyone else. Forgiving others isn’t something we do to earn God’s love — it’s what naturally flows out of hearts that know they’ve been forgiven.

🙏 Prayer

Father, thank you that you have forgiven us so much more than we could ever repay. Thank you for Jesus, who paid our debt on the cross so we could be completely forgiven. Help us to remember how much you have forgiven us, and to forgive others from the heart — even when it’s hard. Give us kind and gentle hearts, just like yours. Amen.

Craft – Unforgiving Servant Cootie Catcher
10 min

Children fold a paper cootie catcher (fortune teller) and use it to retell the parable. Four pictures on top — Forgive, King, Talents, and Servant — open to eight numbered flaps, each hiding a question about the story to lift and answer together.

Before the Session

  • Print one template per child. Use the full-colour page as is, or print one of the black-and-white pages for children to colour first.
  • Print one copy of the folding instructions for yourself — steps to fold it, and how to play.
  • Plain paper folds best; trim each sheet to the square before the session if your children are very young.

Materials

  • Printed cootie catcher template — one per child
  • Scissors
  • Crayons or markers (only if using a black-and-white page)

Steps

  1. Cut out the square and follow the six folding steps on the instructions page.
  2. Slip thumbs and index fingers into the four pockets to open and close it.
  3. In pairs: pick a top picture and spell it, pick a number and count, then pick again and lift the flap.
  4. Read the question under the flap and answer it together.
Tip: The eight flaps hold the story’s key questions — how much the servant owed, how the king responded, what the servant did to his friend, and the main lesson. Let the children answer before you help.
Leader note: Tie it back to the story: the king forgave a debt no one could repay, and Jesus paid our debt on the cross — so we forgive others as God has forgiven us.
